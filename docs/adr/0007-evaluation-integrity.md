# Guard the online-vs-offline evaluation claim against circularity

The Synthetic Merchant Simulator defines ground-truth recovery probability; the decision engine's estimator ([[0006-decision-engine-estimator]]) estimates that same quantity. If the estimator is designed with visibility into the generator's actual response-curve parameters — or, worse, is informed by real Razorpay outcomes that are themselves downstream of the simulator's own roll — then "the online agent captured N% of offline-optimal" measures nothing except that the same assumptions were written down twice. This is the most attackable claim in the project and needs a deliberate, documented defense rather than an implicit hope that it's fine.

**Decision**: Two independence rules apply, at different times. At construction time: the simulator's response-curve generator is written first, in full, before the estimator's priors/feature design; if AI-assisted, that design work happens in a separate session/context that never sees the generator's code or parameter values. At runtime, permanently: the decision engine never sees simulator ground truth at all (Customer Segment, per-case outcome odds), consistent with the existing rule in `CONTEXT.md` — and, per [[0006-decision-engine-estimator]], real Razorpay test-mode executions are excluded from the estimator's evidence entirely, since they're a replay of a decision the simulator already made, not an independent sample. Evaluation uses 300 dev / 150 validation / 200 held-out synthetic cases, never tuned on the held-out set — a size chosen for calibration quality, unrelated to and unconstrained by Razorpay's 30-payment-link test-mode cap (which separately bounds only a small, hand-picked ~20–25-case slice executed through real Razorpay purely to prove integration mechanics, not to produce evaluation statistics). The headline comparison is a paired counterfactual replay: the same case stream, under a shared RNG seed, run through a no-intervention baseline, a fixed-rule baseline (blanket 5% discount), the AI treatment, and a retrospective offline-optimal calculation — net recovered reported with a bootstrap confidence interval on the AI-vs-baseline gap, not a bare point estimate. Before the number is presented as real, a misspecification stress test runs: the simulator's persona mix, response-curve elasticities, and fatigue decay rate are each perturbed by roughly ±20%, the full paired evaluation reruns, and the AI-vs-baseline lift must survive under at least 2 of the 3 perturbations.

**Consequences**: More process discipline up front (write the generator first, keep the estimator blind to it, don't touch the held-out set) and a smaller real-Razorpay-executed slice than the original ~1,000-case demo concept implied — but the resulting numbers are defensible under the exact question ("did you just write your assumptions down twice?") that would otherwise be the fastest way to lose credibility with a payments-literate judge.
