# Pin down the evaluation metric, baseline arms, and bootstrap procedure before ticket 15

[[0007-evaluation-integrity]] already decided *why* the evaluation has to be structured this way (the circularity guard between simulator ground truth and the estimator) and named the headline pieces in passing — the two baselines, the paired replay, a bootstrap CI, the 300/150/200 split. What it doesn't pin down is the operational detail ticket 15 needs to actually build the harness without improvising: the exact per-case metric formula, precisely what each baseline arm does, how "paired" is enforced mechanically, and the concrete bootstrap procedure. This ADR is that pin-down, per ticket 11 — ticket 15 implements against this document directly rather than re-deriving any of it.

**Decision**:

**Primary metric.** Net Recovered Revenue (CONTEXT.md), computed per case as `NRR(case) = gross_recovered(case) − incentive_cost(case)`, where `gross_recovered` is the recovered payment/subscription amount (0 if the case never recovers) and `incentive_cost` is the sum of incentive amounts actually incurred across every intervention executed on that case, regardless of whether it ultimately recovered. A batch's NRR is the sum (not average) of its cases' NRR — it is a total-dollars figure, consistent with Recovery Budget being a total ceiling. This is the metric every arm below is scored on; raw recovery rate is never reported as the headline number.

**The two baselines**, each run as a full arm through the same Policy Engine and Streaming Allocator (same Recovery Budget ceiling, same constraints) as the AI treatment — only the proposed intervention differs between arms, so budget/policy governance is never a confound:
- **No-intervention**: every case is always proposed `NO_ACTION`. `NRR(case)` collapses to `gross_recovered(case)` from the customer's organic (no-nudge) resolution behavior, with zero incentive cost.
- **Fixed-rule**: every case is always proposed its workflow's cost-bearing intervention (`payment_retry` for `failed_payment`, `resume_charge` for `halted_subscription`) at a flat 5% discount — no Decision Engine estimate involved, just the hardcoded rate.

**Offline-optimal** is a fourth arm, computed retrospectively with full visibility into all cases' outcomes, per [[0003-streaming-allocation]] and CONTEXT.md's Offline-Optimal Allocation entry. It is reported separately as `% of offline-optimal NRR captured` by the AI arm. Spec user story 42 names only two things as "baselines" (no-intervention, fixed-rule); offline-optimal is CONTEXT.md's "evaluation baseline" in the loose sense but not one of *the two baselines* the bootstrap gap in [[0007-evaluation-integrity]] is scored against — this is a disambiguation of that ADR's "AI-vs-baseline gap" phrasing, not a revision of its decision.

**Paired counterfactual replay, mechanically.** One synthetic case stream is generated per evaluation run. Each case gets a deterministic per-case seed derived from `(run_seed, case_id)`. All arms (no-intervention, fixed-rule, AI, offline-optimal) replay the identical stream in the identical arrival order; for a given case, each arm's chosen intervention is fed to the same seeded draw in the Synthetic Merchant Simulator's response curve. Holding the seed fixed per case across arms means the only thing that can differ between arms' outcomes for that case is the intervention each arm chose — not independent random noise — which is what makes a direct case-by-case subtraction ("paired") valid.

**Reporting: bootstrap CI on the paired gap.** For each baseline, compute the per-case paired gap `gap(case) = NRR_AI(case) − NRR_baseline(case)` across the evaluation set. Resample these per-case gaps with replacement 10,000 times, take the mean gap per resample, and report the point estimate (mean of the actual gaps) alongside the 95% percentile interval (2.5th–97.5th percentile of the resampled means). This produces two CIs — AI vs. no-intervention, AI vs. fixed-rule — reported separately, never blended into one number.

**Dataset splits.** 300 dev / 150 validation / 200 held-out synthetic cases, generated once under a fixed top-level seed and partitioned by a fixed sequential rule (cases 1–300 dev, 301–450 validation, 451–650 held-out) so the split itself is reproducible from the seed alone. Dev is for iteration during development; validation is for any tuning decision that needs a check against data unseen during dev (e.g. choosing between candidate configs). Held-out is touched exactly once, for the final reported headline comparison (this paired replay + bootstrap CI, plus the [[0007-evaluation-integrity]] misspecification stress test) — it is never used to inform code, priors, or thresholds beforehand.

**Amendment (2026-09-03) — estimator-blind arms and the value-aware allocator gates.** The per-case `case_value` work adds two Streaming Allocator gates that key on the candidate's recovery-probability estimate: an expected-net-value margin gate (`p̂ − uncertainty/2 > incentive_pct`, applied to every funding decision) and the pre-existing reserve quality gate (`p̂ − uncertainty/2 ≥ min_quality_score`). Both are defined only where a `point_estimate` is present. The **fixed-rule** and **no-intervention** arms are estimator-blind by the baseline definitions above ("no Decision Engine estimate involved"), so their `AllocationCandidate.point_estimate` is `None` and neither gate is evaluated for them — a threshold does not apply to an absent field. This is a consequence of those definitions, not a per-arm branch: the gate condition is `point_estimate is not None and …`, never `arm == "fixed_rule"`. The allocator is otherwise unchanged across arms (Recovery Budget ceiling, Reserved Budget mechanism, arrival order), so the non-confound clause above still holds — arms differ only in the proposed intervention and in whether a probability estimate accompanies it.

**Consequences**: Ticket 15 has a concrete formula and procedure to implement against — per-case NRR accounting, two baseline-arm definitions, seeded-pairing mechanics, and exact bootstrap parameters (10,000 resamples, 95% percentile interval) — with no remaining ambiguity to resolve mid-implementation. The 10,000-resample/95%-interval choice is a default, not a constraint from the spec; ticket 15 can adjust it if it finds a concrete reason to, but should record that as a deliberate change against this ADR rather than a silent one. This ADR does not re-litigate [[0007-evaluation-integrity]]'s independence rules (estimator blindness to simulator ground truth, real-execution exclusion from the estimator's evidence) — those still govern how the AI arm's decisions get made in the first place; this ADR only covers how all four arms get compared once decisions are made.
